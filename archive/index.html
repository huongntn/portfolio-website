SAP Clean Core Principles: Compliance Assessment and Transformation Blueprint for SAP HCM at Schwarz Digits

The Challenge: The SAP HCM landscape at Schwarz Digits was hindered by extensive custom legacy code, which caused high maintenance demands, strong architectural coupling, and increased upgrade risks. Because general Clean Core guidelines did not sufficiently address the specific technical complexities of payroll and infotype structures, the organization lacked the necessary framework to assess and prioritize system modernization. Consequently, there was a critical need for a structured classification system and a formal transformation blueprint to guide the long-term stabilization of the core environment.

My Approach: I developed a structured assessment framework based on Design Science Research to modernize the SAP HCM landscape. My approach involved analyzing all transport records from 2023 to 2025 to verify that the 2,928 audited objects represented active, evolving code rather than dead code, as higher change frequency directly correlates to increased risk and technical debt. To ensure a balanced methodology, I integrated SAP-specific extensibility KPIs with McCabe’s cyclomatic complexity metrics to quantify technical debt and architectural risk. Finally, I created a transformation blueprint that provided clear, actionable strategies to refactor, re-platform, or retire legacy code, ensuring long-term system stability.

The Impact: The research provided a repeatable, data-driven framework for modernizing complex SAP environments. By filtering for active objects through a three-year transport analysis, the study helped the organization isolate and prioritize high-risk technical debt, shifting focus away from irrelevant dead code toward areas that actually impact system stability. The integration of SAP-specific extensibility KPIs and academic complexity metrics allowed for a more precise classification of custom objects, enabling the business to make informed decisions on whether to refactor, re-platform, or retire specific components. Ultimately, this transformation blueprint served as a practical governance tool, reducing long-term upgrade risks and establishing a clearer path toward a cleaner, more maintainable SAP HCM architecture.
